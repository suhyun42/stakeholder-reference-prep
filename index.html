01 / In my own words

What the role is about—and where I fit

Helping OpenAI grow and retain its B2B business

My understanding is that this role helps OpenAI identify and evaluate ways to grow usage, revenue, and customer retention across its B2B products. That means looking at pricing, enterprise commitment offers, and product capabilities—and determining which changes are worth pursuing given their growth potential and financial tradeoffs.

A major part of the job seems to be taking an ambiguous business question, figuring out the right drivers and data, building enough structure to compare options, and helping leadership make a decision.

Where I bring relevant experience

Commercial levers and product economics

Pricing, commitment programs, feature economics, and growth-versus-margin tradeoffs are familiar territory for me. I've worked through these questions at Microsoft and know how to connect customer behavior, product strategy, and financial implications.

Where I expect to stretch

More detailed revenue forecasting

The area I'll need to deepen is owning detailed revenue models and projections. At Microsoft, some of that recurring forecasting work sits more directly with Revenue Finance; my own work has focused more on decision-oriented business cases and scenario modeling.

In the interview, I explained that I start with revenue = realized price × usage, then break that into drivers such as customer segments, usage growth, product mix, discounts, and adoption. From there I'd make assumptions explicit, test sensitivities, and refine the model against actual performance.

Where your perspective could be especially helpful

Since you've worked with me on OneLake Reservations and other OneLake/Fabric decisions, your firsthand perspective on how I frame ambiguous questions, build and test financial analyses, work with Finance, and translate the economics into recommendations could be particularly relevant.

I'm not looking for you to say I've run a full Revenue Finance forecasting cycle if you haven't seen that. Examples that show I have the fundamentals, exercise good judgment, and can learn the parts I haven't owned before would be more useful—and more credible. Please speak to whatever you've genuinely observed.

02 / Role specifics

What the role covers in practice

A portfolio-wide strategic finance individual contributor supporting the API Platform and other B2B products. The team translates product and customer activity into financial recommendations that guide growth, profitability, and investment decisions.

01 / Product economics

Connect usage to margin

Model customer and product usage, pricing, discounts, credits, commitments, revenue, compute demand, and contribution margin.

02 / Business decisions

Evaluate growth tradeoffs

Assess enterprise deal economics, monetization choices, customer profitability, growth investments, and resource allocation.

03 / Building 0 → 1

Create financial frameworks

Develop driver-based models, forecasting tools, operating guardrails, and clear narratives for cross-functional leaders.

A simplified view of the work
Customer demand + commercial terms → Revenue + compute costs → Margin + investment decisions

From the interview discussions, Jacob particularly emphasized independent ownership, sound business judgment, and bringing structure to ambiguous problems. These are themes he may want to understand from people who have worked with me.

03 / Background

How I positioned my experience

I'm a Senior Business Planner at Microsoft, leading pricing, packaging, and product economics across the Data and AI portfolio. I've been at Microsoft for about four years, with the last three on the Monetization and Business Planning team.

My work combines strategic decisions—growth, retention, monetization, business cases—with operational execution, including launch implementation, billing meters, and post-launch questions. I partner with Product, Engineering, Finance, Sales, Marketing, and Legal, and present recommendations to senior leadership depending on impact.

Before Microsoft, I spent roughly five years in growth and strategy, focused on customer research and experimentation. I completed my MBA at Cornell to move closer to implementation and financial decision-making.

Why OpenAI: I want deeper ownership of the financial decisions behind scaling B2B products—especially margin tradeoffs, growth investments, and where leadership places bets.
04 / Shared work

Projects we've worked on together

These are project refreshers, not talking points to memorize. Please use only the portions you directly saw or contributed to, and keep internal financial and customer details confidential.

Anchor example · Financial modeling & judgment

OneLake Reservations

We evaluated a new commitment-based reservation offering for enterprise storage customers. The central decision was whether incremental workloads and potential Fabric compute upside justified offering a discount after accounting for existing pay-as-you-go consumption moving into the lower-priced construct.

I framed the business case around incremental storage opportunity, associated compute potential, and migration/cannibalization risk. I used customer signals and historical usage, tested the migration assumption, extended the view across multiple years, and recommended moving forward based on the economics.

Worth reflecting onHow we worked through assumptions, how I involved Finance, whether I independently framed the tradeoffs, and how the analysis supported a business decision.

06 / Discussion guide

Questions Jacob may ask

These are illustrative possibilities, not inside knowledge of the reference process. Expand any question for the kind of firsthand example that could be useful.

How have you worked with Alicia, and what did she own?

Clarify your working relationship and which responsibilities you directly observed. Distinguish her specific contribution from the wider team's work.

How strong is her financial and analytical judgment?

OneLake Reservations is a natural example if you reviewed the business case: framing incrementality versus migration, testing critical assumptions, and turning economics into a recommendation.

Can she structure ambiguous problems independently?

Think about whether she arrives with a clear problem statement, a first-pass model or options, and a recommendation before asking for input.

Can she take on more detailed revenue modeling?

If you've seen the OneLake Reservations model or related analysis, it may help to describe the quality of her assumptions, how she handled uncertain inputs, and how she learned or tested unfamiliar financial questions. There's no need to suggest she has owned recurring Revenue Finance forecasts if that wasn't part of your work together.

How does she balance growth and profitability?

Use a decision you directly saw where product adoption, customer economics, commercial terms, or margin were in tension. Focus on her reasoning rather than on confidential figures.

What's it like collaborating with her?

Consider responsiveness, inclusion, directness, preparation, handling disagreement, and whether she makes your team's work more effective.

How does she influence stakeholders without authority?

Recall a moment where different functions had competing priorities and how she helped clarify tradeoffs or reach alignment.

How does she handle feedback or a changing direction?

A genuine example of listening, revising an analysis, adjusting a recommendation, or learning from a difficult decision is helpful.

Where can she continue growing?

Share your candid perspective, preferably with an observed example and progress you have seen. My own reflection areas include sharper focus on highest-impact work and tailoring stakeholder input to the decision—but your independent assessment is more important.

Would you choose to work with her again?

It's useful to be prepared with your real assessment of why you would—or any relevant qualification. Concrete reasons carry more weight than a generic endorsement.
